BOH4M — Business Leadership: Management Fundamentals

Leading by Design

Designing systems that help people work with confidence.

I

Foundation

Components 01 – 03

01

Leadership Philosophy

Leadership begins by designing the conditions in which others can succeed.

Operating principle

Leadership is the work of designing systems that make other people's work easier, rather than relying on authority to direct them.

Design the conditions

01

Academic decision

When I asked my advisor to delay the test, I chose understanding over short-term preparation. I had been memorizing procedures without understanding their logic, so I returned to algebra fundamentals and rebuilt my knowledge through practice. That foundation helped me progress into functions, where tools such as Desmos showed me that graphs can explain behaviour and support prediction, not merely produce an answer. The experience taught me to address the structure of a problem instead of hiding it with a temporary result.

02

System design

During my internship at Mindstreams Education, I found that staff had no automated system for issuing documents. Before building one, I researched the relevant UK legal requirements and treated GDPR compliance as a design constraint. I then presented my findings to the company's owner, whose technical and business knowledge helped refine the process. We reviewed the system together before publication. The experience showed me that leadership can mean preparing a sound structure, inviting informed challenge, and improving the design before asking others to rely on it.

03

Development edge

I have explained ideas and concepts to people who were interested and willing to learn. However, I have never had to lead someone who was disengaged from the work or opposed to what I was trying to accomplish. In every context I have worked in so far, the people involved already wanted the outcome, so I have never had to create motivation where it was not already present.

02

Leadership Quote

The strongest influence leaves people capable, not dependent.

Interpretation

What struck me about this passage was the idea that meaningful work can be almost completely invisible. At Mindstreams Education, the company's owner worked directly with tutors while I supported the process in the background. I made technical changes, solved problems, and adjusted the system so tutors could continue their work more easily, even though they did not know who had created the improvements.

Lao Tzu's passage therefore feels real to me because leadership can be effective when people experience capability rather than dependence. I value contributing quietly, but I also recognize that complete invisibility can make sustained effort difficult. The quote matters to me because it captures both the ideal I want to pursue and a tension I still need to manage: helping others claim the outcome while accepting that I sometimes want my own contribution to be acknowledged.

03

Vision and Mission

Technology becomes leadership when it expands what others can do.

Vision

To become a proactive and influential leader who uses technology, innovation, and leadership to improve the way people live and work. I envision leading talented teams, driving meaningful organizational change, and potentially building my own business while expanding my impact from my local community to the wider world. Ultimately, I aspire to create environments where people feel empowered to embrace technology and use it confidently to solve real-world challenges.

Local impact Wider impact

Mission

My mission is to use my knowledge of computer science, technology, and other STEM fields to develop practical solutions that improve people's lives and working environments. I aim to make technology more accessible and less intimidating, especially for individuals who may lack technical experience or confidence. Through continuous learning, innovation, leadership, and collaboration, I will empower others, build strong teams, and create positive change that begins within my community and grows to have a broader impact.

Direction

My direction begins locally: using technology to solve practical problems and reduce the apprehension of people who lack technical confidence. Over time, I want to extend that work across organizations and fields, potentially including biotechnology and other STEM areas. Within fifteen years, I aim to hold a senior leadership role, guide a technical team, and possibly establish a business. The common purpose is to combine computer science with leadership so that useful ideas become systems people can confidently use.

Goals and Plans

Purpose becomes progress through specific, measurable commitments.

To reach this, I will complete at least three full-length timed practice tests before the sitting and concentrate the majority of my remaining preparation on Reading and Writing, which my diagnostic identified as the weaker of the two sections, while continuing to maintain and strengthen Mathematics.

    Within five years I will complete a bachelor's degree in computer science, including the preparatory year required by Saudi programs, and secure at least one internship or freelance engagement in each year of study. Academic programs emphasize theory, and my internship at Mindstreams Education showed me how much of professional competence comes from applied work, so I want practical experience accumulating alongside my studies rather than only afterward.

05

Personal Leadership Development

Growth means learning to lead through people, not only through systems.

i

The clearest evidence of my leadership development came from the Time Management Profile I completed in February. I scored 9 out of 12, but the most important result was identifying that I did not delegate tasks that someone else could complete. My awareness of this problem has changed faster than my behaviour. I still tend to take responsibility for the entire solution, especially when I understand the technical work. I now recognize that this limits the scale of what I can accomplish and can make other people dependent on me.

Servant leadership fits my approach because I measure success by whether other people can work more easily and independently. I prefer to remove obstacles, clarify a process, and leave behind something useful rather than rely on authority. This tendency also explains the portfolio's "Leading by Design" theme: I try to shape the conditions around work so that good performance becomes easier. However, servant leadership does not fit me perfectly. I often solve problems independently, so I still need experience delegating responsibility, coaching others, and adjusting my approach when support alone is not enough.

ii

At the beginning of the course, I associated leadership mainly with formal authority and directing people. I now understand that it can also operate through expertise, preparation, communication, and system design. My instinct has often been to make myself unnecessary by documenting a process. That can preserve knowledge, but documentation is not the same as delegation because it transfers instructions after the main decisions have already been made. Leading through people requires involving someone earlier, sharing judgement, accepting a different method, and remaining accountable without reclaiming control. This distinction has changed how I evaluate my own work.

Online schooling since Grade 9 has influenced this development. It gave me considerable independence and experience managing projects on my own, but it provided no opportunities to lead school teams in person. Most people I have worked with already wanted the outcome or were willing to learn. I have not yet had to motivate someone who was disengaged or strongly opposed to my approach. This is an important limitation because designing a good system does not guarantee that people will accept or use it.

iii

My next stage of development is learning to lead through other people rather than only through what I can build myself. In my next group or technical project, I will delegate at least one lower-risk responsibility, define the required outcome and deadline, agree on checkpoints, and allow the other person to choose the method. I will resist correcting every difference that does not affect the result and ask for feedback afterward about whether my guidance was clear. Repeating this process will give me evidence of progress and help me practise trust, coaching, and accountability rather than using documentation as a substitute for collaboration.

Leadership matters to me because technical knowledge becomes more valuable when it helps other people work accurately and independently. A well-designed solution has limited value if it depends on one person. My goal is to create conditions in which other people can carry an idea forward, question it, and improve it.

06

Management Skills

Management turns intention into coordinated, measurable action.

Planning

Scoping the Mindstreams System

Mindstreams Education

Mindstreams Education had no automated document process, so I mapped what tutors submitted, what management needed to receive, and how the website and back-end tools could connect. Because the company operates in the United Kingdom, I treated GDPR compliance as a design constraint from the start. This was planning because I defined the problem, objectives, technical dependencies, and legal boundaries before choosing a solution. The scoping work taught me that a reliable system begins with requirements, not code.

Course Planner Needs Analysis

Personal project

Available planners were either too generic or depended heavily on spreadsheets, so I defined the need for one place to organize schedules, lessons, assignments, and resources. I decided that the first version would reproduce the useful structure of my spreadsheet before adding advanced features. This was planning through needs analysis and objective-setting. Establishing a clear purpose gave the project boundaries and let me judge each proposed feature by whether it improved the planner's usefulness.

Planning the Portfolio Site

This portfolio

Before building this portfolio, I translated the assignment into a nine-section brief and compared a LinkedIn profile, blog, slide presentation, and website. I chose a single-scroll site because it could hold the required reflections while numbered navigation kept each component discoverable. I also defined the "Leading by Design" theme and a light editorial visual direction before development. This demonstrates planning because the audience, structure, format, and design criteria were decided before the site was built.

Organizing

Document-Automation Workflow

Mindstreams Education

The Mindstreams automation required several tools to operate as one process. Tutors enter information through Wix forms; Wix Automation transfers it to a spreadsheet; Google Apps Script generates the required document; and the system emails, converts, and stores the file using a consistent name. This demonstrates organizing because I assigned each component a specific role and arranged their dependencies into a coordinated workflow. It developed my ability to turn separate technical tools into a reliable business process.

Building the Planner in Versions

Personal project

I organized the Course Planner into versions instead of attempting every feature at once. The first reproduced the essential structure of my spreadsheet and created a working baseline; later versions could improve features without replacing the foundation. I also separated schedules, lessons, assignments, and resources so each type of information had a clear location. This was organizing because I divided a large project into manageable components and established their order. The approach kept the planner usable while it continued to develop.

Mapping the Nvidia Project

Coursework

For my Nvidia project, I mapped how the script, slides, and recording depended on one another. The script determined what each slide needed to communicate, while the slide order shaped the recording's structure and transitions. This was organizing because I arranged related tasks by dependency and decided whether information belonged in narration or on screen. The map reduced repetition, exposed missing visuals, and helped the finished parts support one coherent presentation.

Leading

Influencing a Technical Decision Without Authority

Mindstreams Education

While developing the Mindstreams system, I researched the legal requirements and presented my findings to the company's owner. Her computer-engineering background and knowledge of operations let her challenge both the technical and business sides of my work. We revised and reviewed the system together before publication. This demonstrates leading through expert influence and collaborative decision-making rather than formal authority. I learned that good leadership can mean bringing evidence into a discussion and allowing another person's expertise to improve the result.

Tutor Instruction Manual

Mindstreams Education

After building the Mindstreams forms, I wrote a manual explaining how tutors should use them and when to use the manual fallback. The document translated a technical process into instructions that employees could follow without the developer being present. This demonstrates leading through communication and knowledge sharing. It developed my ability to explain a system from the user's perspective and created a process that could continue without requiring me to repeat every instruction personally.

Supporting My Father Remotely

Family

When visa issues required me to leave the country, I could no longer help my father directly with parts of his work. I created a step-by-step manual explaining the workflow and the decisions involved, which he used to continue while I was away. This demonstrated leading through preparation and knowledge transfer. It also challenged my habit of remaining the person who solves everything by showing that clear guidance can help someone else work independently.

Controlling

Preventive GDPR Control

Mindstreams Education

After identifying GDPR compliance as a planning constraint, I used the requirements as preventive control standards throughout development. I checked how information would be collected, transferred, used, and stored instead of waiting until the system was complete. The company's owner then reviewed and adjusted the process using her business knowledge. This demonstrates controlling because compliance was built into the workflow before publication. I learned that early standards can prevent expensive corrections or a complete rebuild later.

Back-End Verification and Error Hardening

Mindstreams Education

After connecting the Wix forms, spreadsheet, and Google Apps Script, I tested the complete workflow. I verified that information reached the spreadsheet, the script generated the correct document, the email was sent, and the PDF used the expected name. When errors or unusual inputs appeared, I revised the back-end logic. This demonstrates controlling because I compared actual output with a defined standard and made corrective changes. Repeated testing developed my attention to reliability rather than merely whether a feature worked once.

Managing My Passport Renewal

Personal responsibility

I managed my passport renewal by reviewing the official requirements, gathering the government documents, completing the application, and checking every detail before submission. My parents only verified that the personal information was accurate. I treated the stated requirements as a control standard because missing information could delay the process. This demonstrates controlling through monitoring and preventive correction. It developed my ability to manage an unfamiliar administrative process and take responsibility for accuracy before another organization reviewed my work.

07

Leader Idol

A leadership model should be studied critically, not copied completely.

Jensen Huang

Co-founder, President and Chief Executive Officer, NVIDIA Corporation

Jensen Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and has remained its president and chief executive officer through major shifts in computing ("Jensen Huang"). His persistence is notable, but I chose him because he treats organizational structure as something a leader can deliberately redesign rather than simply inherit.

Huang combines transformational leadership with participative problem-solving. He brings different technical specialists into the same discussion so they can contribute expertise and hear the reasoning behind decisions. In 2026, he described having roughly 60 direct reports and replacing individual meetings with group discussions in which the team practises "extreme co-design" (Shibu). This creates an exceptionally wide span of control and shortens Fayol's scalar chain between the chief executive and technical decision-makers. The approach is participative because employees help solve the problem, yet authority remains centralized because so many reporting relationships lead directly to Huang. It is transformational because the organization is designed around agility, shared information, and long-term technological change rather than a conventional hierarchy.

This structure connects with my belief that leadership involves designing conditions that help other people work effectively. Earlier reporting described Huang's goal as moving information quickly and empowering employees through what they can do, not through information available only to them (Royle). I admire the transparency of that principle. My own style is quieter and more servant-oriented, but the shared idea is that useful knowledge should strengthen the group rather than become a private source of influence.

I would emulate Huang's use of shared context, direct access to expertise, and cross-functional problem-solving because these practices can reduce distortion and unnecessary delay. I would not copy the model completely. Removing regular one-on-one meetings can eliminate a private channel for discussing performance, conflict, or a sensitive concern, while approximately 60 direct reports limit individual coaching. Huang has also described working every waking moment, seven days a week, and acknowledged the anxiety and exhaustion involved (Shibu). That intensity may suit a founder with decades of experience, but it is not a sustainable expectation I would impose on myself or others. His example matters because it shows that leadership design must match the organization, its people, and the situation.

08

Personality Traits

Self-awareness shapes where and how I contribute best.

INTJ-A

Architect (Assertive)

My Myers-Briggs result is INTJ-A, the Assertive Architect. I recognize its independence, analytical thinking, long-term planning, and preference for relying on personal judgement. I concentrate best in quiet, structured environments where I can analyse information and improve systems, while extended social interaction drains rather than energizes me. This does not prevent me from leading; it means I contribute most effectively when I have time to prepare, understand the problem, and enter collaboration with a clear purpose.

I offer the ability to analyze an incomplete process and build a practical system around it. At Mindstreams Education, I developed a document-automation workflow while accounting for UK GDPR requirements, wrote a manual so tutors could operate the forms without me, and included a manual fallback in case the automation failed. This demonstrates technical ability, preventive planning, documentation, quality control, and an awareness that a useful system must remain reliable after its creator steps away.

I prefer a less traditional working environment. Fixed cubicle arrangements do not suit how I work best. I would rather be in an open culture where employees have the freedom to manage their own work and choose where they do it, whether that means working from an office, from home, or elsewhere. That flexibility supports concentration and creativity, and it matches the way I have studied and worked independently since Grade 9.

Suited Careers

  1. AI or machine-learning engineer

    It combines programming with building intelligent systems, and the underlying mathematics is a part of the work I genuinely enjoy. The role would let me investigate a technical problem deeply before explaining the result to a wider team.

  2. Data scientist

    It rewards analytical reasoning and identifying patterns within complex information, which matches how I approach problems. I would also value turning those findings into evidence that helps an organization make a clearer decision.

  3. IT systems manager

    It involves designing, documenting, and controlling organizational technology—the same combination I practised at Mindstreams Education. It would also require me to develop the delegation and communication skills that independent technical work does not exercise as fully.

  4. Strategic consultant

    It focuses on diagnosing the underlying problem rather than treating its symptoms, which is how I approach systems. Presenting recommendations to clients would challenge me to make technical reasoning persuasive and useful to people with different priorities.

  5. Research or business analyst

    It requires independent investigation and evidence-based recommendations, both of which suit how I work. The role would still require concise communication so that research becomes a decision rather than remaining an isolated analysis.

MBTI describes my current preferences and working tendencies, but it does not determine my abilities or reliably predict my performance in a particular career.

09

Ideal Employer

The right employer aligns meaningful work, personal values, and measurable impact.

Shopify Inc.

Ottawa, Ontario

0 words · target 250–500

My ideal Canadian employer is Shopify, a commerce technology company headquartered in Ottawa, Ontario. I chose it not because every decision is beyond criticism, but because its strongest initiatives use technology and market design to help employees, entrepreneurs, and climate-focused businesses build.

Employees

Shopify's "Digital by Design" model lets employees complete daily work wherever they perform best while bringing teams together for onboarding, company gatherings, and short in-person "Bursts." Shopify describes this culture as based on trust rather than location ("Careers"). The balance suits my experience studying independently online, while planned collaboration would help me learn from experienced technical teams instead of remaining isolated.

Community

Shopify contributes to communities through entrepreneurship. Its partnership with Operation HOPE supports the 1 Million Black Businesses initiative, which provides education, coaching, and business-development support. Operation HOPE reported that the initiative had engaged more than 469,000 people by December 2025 and identified Shopify as its partner through that reporting period (HOPE Impact Report 2). The same report recorded more than 531,000 cumulative small-business development services from October 2020 through December 2025. These results make the commitment measurable rather than leaving it as a general statement of support.

Environment

The company's environmental work is the strongest reason for my choice. Shopify reports committing more than $100 million to climate entrepreneurs since 2019 ("2025 Climate Report"). It also helped establish Frontier, whose founding companies committed a combined $925 million to purchase permanent carbon removal through 2030 (Ross). By guaranteeing future demand, the initiative helps early-stage suppliers finance and scale technologies before the market is mature. This does more than compensate for existing emissions: it creates demand for solutions that might otherwise struggle to attract early investment. That matches my leadership philosophy because it changes the structure around a problem rather than treating only its visible effects.

Shopify describes its workplace as fast-paced and demanding, so it would challenge my time management and tendency to take on too much myself ("Careers"). Even so, its combination of independent digital work, difficult technical problems, entrepreneurship, and system-level environmental action aligns closely with both my interests and the leader I am trying to become.
