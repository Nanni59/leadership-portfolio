01

Leadership Philosophy

[theme line]

Leadership is the work of designing systems that make other people's work easier, rather than relying on authority to direct them.

01

When I asked for the delay, I was unsure how my advisor would respond or whether the request would even be accepted. My first attempt had been to simply study for the test, but I realized I was memorizing material without understanding the logic behind it. I chose to use time that could have gone toward exam preparation to rebuild my understanding of the subject instead. I went back to the fundamentals and worked my way up through practice problems. Strengthening my understanding of algebra gave me the foundation to move into functions, and when I began using Desmos, I realized there was more to functions than simply solving equations. Graphs could show how a function behaved and help predict information from a problem rather than only produce an answer.

02

During my internship at Mindstreams Education, the problem was not that its document process was broken, but that there was no system in place for automating documents issued by staff. Because I was unfamiliar with UK law, I researched the relevant legal requirements before building the system. Designing those requirements into the process from the start mattered because a system created without GDPR compliance could have required significant changes or even rebuilding later. I brought my findings to the company's owner, who has a background in computer engineering, and she adjusted sections based on her knowledge of the business. We then reviewed the system together before publishing it on the website.

03

I have explained ideas and concepts to people who were interested and willing to learn. However, I have never had to lead someone who was disengaged from the work or opposed to what I was trying to accomplish. In every context I have worked in so far, the people involved already wanted the outcome, so I have never had to create motivation where it was not already present.

02

Leadership Quote

[theme line]

What struck me about this passage was the idea that meaningful work can be almost completely invisible. A person can shape an outcome without the people benefiting from it ever knowing who was behind it.

That idea reminded me of the work I did supporting my aunt and the tutors she worked with. My aunt was the person who interacted with the tutors directly; I was entirely in the background as IT support. The tutors did not know I existed. However, many of the changes they experienced came from work I was doing behind the scenes. I was the one making the technical changes, fixing problems, and adjusting the systems so that things worked more smoothly for them. From their perspective, the improvements simply appeared. They could continue their work without seeing the person responsible for making those changes possible.

That experience makes Lao Tzu's passage feel very real to me, although I find the invisibility harder than the passage makes it sound. There is something satisfying about knowing that my work helped people without requiring recognition. At the same time, being completely unseen can be difficult. I can appreciate the value of contributing quietly while also admitting that sometimes I want my effort—and not only the final result—to be acknowledged.

03

Vision and Mission

[theme line]

Vision

To become a proactive and influential leader who uses technology, innovation, and leadership to improve the way people live and work. I envision leading talented teams, driving meaningful organizational change, and potentially building my own business while expanding my impact from my local community to the wider world. Ultimately, I aspire to create environments where people feel empowered to embrace technology and use it confidently to solve real-world challenges.

Local impact Wider impact

Mission

My mission is to use my knowledge of computer science, technology, and other STEM fields to develop practical solutions that improve people's lives and working environments. I aim to make technology more accessible and less intimidating, especially for individuals who may lack technical experience or confidence. Through continuous learning, innovation, leadership, and collaboration, I will empower others, build strong teams, and create positive change that begins within my community and grows to have a broader impact.

My ambition begins with effecting change within my local community, then broadening that impact to the city, the country, and ultimately as far as progress allows. The underlying purpose is to improve people's lives, reduce their apprehension toward technology, and make it accessible to those who do not already possess technical skills. This direction is not confined to a single field; it could extend into biotechnology, the sciences, and other STEM areas. Within fifteen years I see myself in a senior leadership role, leading a team, driving organizational change, and potentially establishing my own business, using my expertise in computer science to improve real-world operations rather than working in isolation.

05

Personal Leadership Development

[theme line]

i

The clearest evidence of my leadership development came from the Time Management Profile I completed in February. I scored 9 out of 12, but the most important result was identifying that I did not delegate tasks that someone else could complete. My awareness of this problem has changed faster than my behaviour. I still tend to take responsibility for the entire solution, especially when I understand the technical work. I now recognize that this limits the scale of what I can accomplish and can make other people dependent on me.

Servant leadership fits my approach best because I usually try to make other people's work easier. At Mindstreams Education, I built a document system that reduced the manual formatting tutors previously had to complete. I also wrote a manual so they could use the forms without needing me to explain the process each time. This reflects servant leadership because the focus was on removing obstacles for other people rather than gaining authority over them. However, the style does not fit me perfectly. I often work independently, and I still need experience delegating responsibilities and developing other people's abilities through direct collaboration.

ii

At the beginning of the course, I associated leadership mainly with formal authority and directing people. I now understand that leadership can also operate through expertise, preparation, communication, and system design. A person does not need a management title to influence how work is completed. For example, when visa issues required me to leave the country, I created a manual that helped my father continue his work while I was away. I was not supervising him directly, but the guidance I prepared allowed my knowledge to remain useful in my absence. My instinct has often been to make myself unnecessary by writing a process down rather than by handing part of the work to another person. That transfers knowledge successfully when someone is willing to follow it, but it does not teach me how to motivate someone who is uninterested or resistant.

Online schooling since Grade 9 has influenced this development. It gave me considerable independence and experience managing projects on my own, but it provided no opportunities to lead school teams in person. Most people I have worked with already wanted the outcome or were willing to learn. I have not yet had to motivate someone who was disengaged or strongly opposed to my approach. This is an important limitation because designing a good system does not guarantee that people will accept or use it.

iii

My next stage of development is therefore learning to lead through other people rather than only through what I can build myself. I need to delegate lower-risk responsibilities, define the required outcome, and allow someone else to choose how to reach it.

Leadership matters to me because technical knowledge becomes more valuable when it helps other people work accurately and independently. My goal is to become someone who can both design an effective solution and create the conditions in which other people can carry it forward.

06

Management Skills

[theme line]

Planning

01 – 03

01Scoping the Mindstreams System

Mindstreams Education

Before building the document automation system at Mindstreams Education, I first had to understand what the business required. There was no existing automated process, so I examined what information tutors needed to submit, what documents management needed to receive, and how the website and back-end tools could connect. Because Mindstreams operates in the United Kingdom, I also identified GDPR compliance as a design constraint during this scoping stage. This demonstrates planning because I defined the problem, objectives, technical connections, and legal boundaries before deciding how to build the system.

02Course Planner Needs Analysis

Personal project

I created the Course Planner because the available options were either too generic or depended heavily on spreadsheets. Before developing it, I defined the main need: one place where I could organize schedules, lessons, assignments, and learning resources. I decided that the first version should reproduce the useful structure of my spreadsheet before adding more advanced features. This was planning through needs analysis and objective-setting. Establishing that purpose gave the project boundaries and allowed me to evaluate each proposed feature according to whether it improved the planner's usefulness.

03Planning the Portfolio Site

This portfolio

Before building my leadership portfolio site, I translated the assignment into a nine-section content brief and compared several possible formats, including a LinkedIn profile, a blog, and a slide presentation. I chose a single-scroll website because it gave the longer written sections enough space while numbered navigation would let a marker move directly between requirements. I also selected the "Leading by Design" theme and Roboto Condensed font before development began. This demonstrates planning because the format, audience, structure, and visual direction were specified before any code was written.

Organizing

04 – 06

04Document-Automation Workflow

Mindstreams Education

The Mindstreams automation required several tools to operate as one organized process. Tutors enter client information through forms on the Wix website. Wix Automation transfers that information to a spreadsheet, where an embedded Google Apps Script detects the change and generates the required document. The system then emails the document to the company, converts it into a PDF, and stores it in Google Drive using a consistent naming convention. This demonstrates organizing because I assigned a specific role to each component and arranged them into a coordinated workflow that replaced inconsistent manual formatting.

05Building the Planner in Versions

Personal project

I organized development of the Course Planner into versions instead of attempting every feature at once. The first version reproduced the essential structure of the spreadsheet I already used, giving me a working baseline. Later versions could then add or improve features without changing the project's foundation. I also separated schedules, lessons, assignments, and resources so that each type of information had a clear location. This was organizing because I divided a large technical project into manageable components and established an order for completing them. As a result, the project remained usable while it continued to develop.

06Mapping the Nvidia Project

Coursework

The mind map for my Nvidia project showed that the slides, script, and recording were not independent tasks. The script determined what each slide needed to communicate, while the slide order established the structure and transitions of the recording. Mapping these relationships helped me identify where a visual required explanation and where information belonged in the script rather than on the screen. This demonstrates organizing because I arranged related tasks according to their dependencies. Without the map, I could have repeated information, omitted necessary visuals, or completed slides that did not match the final script.

Leading

07 – 09

07Influencing a Technical Decision Without Authority

Mindstreams Education

While developing the Mindstreams system, I researched the relevant legal requirements and presented my findings to the company's owner. She has a background in computer engineering and knowledge of the company's operations, so she was able to evaluate both the technical and business sides of my work. She adjusted certain sections, and we reviewed the system together before publishing it. This demonstrates leading through expert influence and collaborative decision-making rather than formal authority. My research helped shape the final approach, while her review ensured that the system reflected the organization's actual requirements.

08Tutor Instruction Manual

Mindstreams Education

After building the Mindstreams forms, I wrote a company manual explaining how tutors should use them. A system would not improve the organization if employees could not operate it without its developer being present. The manual translated the technical process into instructions that other people could follow and also documented the availability of manual forms as a fallback. This demonstrates leading because I guided other people's work through clear communication and knowledge sharing. The result was a process that could continue without requiring me to personally explain every step to each tutor.

09Supporting My Father Remotely

Family

My father usually relies on me for parts of his work rather than completing the process independently. When visa issues required me to leave the country, I knew I would not be available to help him directly. I created a step-by-step manual explaining the workflow and the decisions involved. He used it while I was away, and it helped him continue his work despite my absence. This demonstrated leadership through preparation and knowledge transfer: instead of remaining the person who needed to solve everything, I created guidance that allowed someone else to keep working.

Controlling

10 – 12

10Preventive GDPR Control

Mindstreams Education

After identifying GDPR compliance as a constraint during planning, I used the relevant requirements as preventive control standards throughout development. Rather than waiting until the system was finished to check compliance, I considered how information would be collected, transferred, used, and stored while building the workflow. I brought my findings to the company's owner, who adjusted sections based on her knowledge of the business, and we reviewed the system together. This demonstrates controlling because compliance was built into the process so that major corrections or a complete rebuild would not be required later.

11Back-End Verification and Error Hardening

Mindstreams Education

After connecting the Wix forms, spreadsheet, and Google Apps Script, I tested the complete workflow rather than assuming that each component would operate correctly. I checked whether submitted information reached the spreadsheet, whether the script generated the correct document, whether the email was sent, and whether the PDF was stored under the expected name. When errors or unusual inputs appeared, I revised the back-end logic to handle them more reliably. This demonstrates controlling because I compared actual output with the expected standard and made corrective changes. The process became more dependable through repeated testing and adjustment.

12Managing My Passport Renewal

Personal responsibility

I managed my own passport renewal rather than leaving the process to my parents. I reviewed the official requirements, gathered the necessary government documents, completed the application, and checked the information before submission. My parents' role was limited to verifying that the personal details were accurate. Government procedures can be delayed when information is incomplete or a required document is missing, so I treated the stated requirements as the standard for reviewing my work. This demonstrates controlling because I monitored the process and corrected potential omissions before they could cause a delay.

07

Leader Idol

[theme line]

JH

Jensen Huang

Co-founder, President and Chief Executive Officer, NVIDIA Corporation

Researching Nvidia earlier in this course first drew my attention to Jensen Huang, but my interest in him extends beyond the company's financial success. Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and has remained its president and chief executive officer since its creation ("Jensen Huang"). Remaining responsible for one technology company through several major changes in computing demonstrates persistence, but what interests me most is how he has deliberately designed Nvidia's management structure.

Huang's approach combines transformational leadership with participative problem-solving. He encourages people from different technical specialties to examine problems together, which supports collaboration and exposes employees to the same reasoning. In 2026, he described having roughly 60 direct reports and replacing individual meetings with group discussions in which the team practises "extreme co-design" (Shibu). In management terms, this gives Huang an exceptionally wide span of control. It also shortens Fayol's scalar chain by removing management layers between him and technical decision-makers. His method is participative because people contribute their expertise to a shared discussion, but authority remains highly centralized because so many reporting relationships lead directly to him. The transformational element is his willingness to redesign the organization around agility, shared information, and a long-term technological vision instead of following a conventional hierarchy.

Huang's system connects with my philosophy that leadership involves designing structures that make other people's work easier. His meetings are intended to prevent information from becoming a form of private influence. Earlier reporting described his goal as allowing information to move quickly and empowering employees through what they can do rather than through information available only to them (Royle). I admire this because the structure itself encourages transparency. In my own work at Mindstreams Education, I also tried to place knowledge inside a system by automating a document process and writing instructions that allowed other people to use it without my presence. However, my approach is closer to servant leadership: I normally work quietly through documentation and support, while Huang leads through highly visible, fast, and demanding group reasoning.

I would emulate Huang's use of shared context, direct access to expertise, and cross-functional problem-solving. These practices could reduce distortion and prevent useful information from being delayed by unnecessary hierarchy. I would not copy his model completely. Removing regular one-on-one meetings can eliminate a private channel where someone might discuss performance, conflict, or a sensitive concern. A span of approximately 60 direct reports also limits the individual coaching a leader can provide. Huang has described working every waking moment, seven days a week, and has acknowledged the anxiety and exhaustion involved (Shibu). That intensity may be possible for a founder with decades of experience, but it is not a sustainable model I would want to impose on myself or other people. His example is therefore meaningful to me not because it offers a structure to copy, but because it demonstrates that leadership design must match the organization, its people, and the situation.

08

Personality Traits

[theme line]

INTJ-A

Architect (Assertive)

My Myers-Briggs type is INTJ-A, the Assertive Architect. Architects are independent, analytical, and determined, valuing rationality and long-term planning, and tend to recognize patterns that others overlook. The Assertive variant is self-assured and comparatively resistant to stress, preferring to rely on its own judgement. I recognize this in how I work: I concentrate best in quiet, structured environments where I can analyse information and improve systems, and I am drained rather than energized by extended social interaction. A common misconception is that introversion means shyness or social difficulty; in practice it describes how a person recovers energy rather than how capable they are of leading.

I offer the ability to analyze an incomplete process and build a practical system around it. At Mindstreams Education, I developed a document-automation workflow while accounting for UK GDPR requirements, wrote a manual so tutors could operate the forms without me, and included a manual fallback in case the automation failed. This demonstrates technical ability, preventive planning, documentation, quality control, and an awareness that a useful system must remain reliable after its creator steps away.

I prefer a less traditional working environment. Fixed cubicle arrangements do not suit how I work best. I would rather be in an open culture where employees have the freedom to manage their own work and choose where they do it, whether that means working from an office, from home, or elsewhere. That flexibility supports concentration and creativity, and it matches the way I have studied and worked independently since Grade 9.

Suited Careers

  1. AI or machine-learning engineer

    It combines programming with building intelligent systems, and the underlying mathematics is the part of the work I find genuinely interesting rather than merely tolerable.

  2. Data scientist

    It rewards analytical reasoning and the identification of patterns within complex information, which matches how I prefer to approach problems.

  3. IT systems manager

    It involves designing, documenting, and controlling organizational technology — the same combination of tasks I carried out during my internship at Mindstreams Education.

  4. Strategic consultant

    It focuses on diagnosing the underlying problem rather than treating its symptoms, which is the approach I take to systems I build for myself and for others.

  5. Research or business analyst

    It requires independent investigation and evidence-based recommendations, both of which suit working alone on a problem before presenting conclusions.

MBTI describes my current preferences and working tendencies, but it does not determine my abilities or reliably predict my performance in a particular career.

Ideal Employer

[theme line]

Shopify Inc.

Ottawa, Ontario

0 words · target 250–500

My ideal Canadian employer is Shopify, a Canadian commerce technology company with its head office in Ottawa, Ontario. I previously studied its management practices, but I am considering it here through a different lens: its responsibility to employees, communities, and the natural environment. Shopify is not ideal because every decision it makes is beyond criticism. It is ideal because its strongest initiatives use technology and market design to help other people build.

Employees

For employees, Shopify's "Digital by Design" model allows people to complete their daily work wherever they perform best, while still bringing teams together for onboarding, company gatherings, and short in-person "Bursts" when a problem benefits from direct collaboration. Shopify describes this culture as based on trust rather than location ("Careers"). This flexibility suits how I have studied and worked since Grade 9. I am comfortable operating independently online, but I would still benefit from planned opportunities to collaborate with experienced technical teams.

Community

Shopify also contributes to communities through entrepreneurship. Its partnership with Operation HOPE supports the 1 Million Black Businesses initiative, which provides education, coaching, and business-development support. Operation HOPE's 2025 report states that the initiative engaged more than 469,000 people and delivered over 531,000 small-business development services cumulatively from October 2020 through December 2025 (HOPE Impact Report 2). The same report identifies Shopify as the initiative's partner through the end of that reporting period. These figures do not show that every earlier Shopify social-impact program remained unchanged, but they document measurable community outcomes rather than relying only on a public commitment.

Environment

The company's environmental work is the strongest reason for my choice. Shopify reports that it has committed more than $100 million to climate entrepreneurs since 2019 ("2025 Climate Report"). It also helped establish Frontier with Stripe, Alphabet, Meta, and McKinsey Sustainability. The founding companies committed a combined $925 million to purchase permanent carbon removal through 2030 (Ross). An advance market commitment does more than compensate for existing emissions: it guarantees future demand so that early-stage suppliers can finance, test, and scale technologies that do not yet have mature markets. This resembles my own leadership philosophy because it addresses the structure producing a problem rather than only its visible effects.

Shopify also describes its workplace as fast-paced and demanding, with growth expected rather than treated as optional ("Careers"). That culture could challenge my time management and tendency to take on too much myself. However, its combination of independent digital work, difficult technical problems, entrepreneurship, and system-level environmental action makes it the organization where my interests and my developing leadership approach align most closely.
